PURPOSE

These policies and procedures are designed to act as a guideline for business travel and
entertainment expense and miscellaneous expense charges/reimbursements. They are intended to
keep pace with industry best practices while balancing concern for traveler safety and convenience.
It is the Company's intent that travel and entertainment expenses be limited through careful
management and appropriate judgment. It is the Company's policy to reimburse employees for all
necessary and reasonable expenses incurred while traveling on Company business or entertaining
business clients.

ALL EXPENSES INCURRED MUST BE IN ACCORDANCE WITH YOUR INDIVIDUAL
DEPARTMENT BUDGET AND DIVISION POLICY. IF YOU ARE UNCLEAR AS TO YOUR
EXPENSE BUDGET OR DIVISION POLICY , PLEASE CONTACT YOUR DEPARTMENT
HEAD.

SCOPE OF POLICY

This policy applies to all employees of Standex International Corporation. ("the Company") and
all divisions and subsidiaries thereof.

POLICY PRINCIPLES

Employees should use their best judgment when traveling on company business and make expense
decisions with the company's best interests in mind and in a manner, consistent with the spirit of
the policy.
Travel expenditures should be consistent with business needs.
Standex Corporate Credit Card must be used always, unless not available to the business traveler.
Any exceptions must be documented when submitting the expense reimbursement request.
Employees are responsible to report lost cards, any unauthorized use or possible comprises to their
credit card directly to Bank of America using the number on the back of their credit card. The
Global Card Services number is 1.888.449.2273; or call collect Outside the U.S. and Canada
1.602.379.8753.
The Company has the right to question any expe nses and will withhold expense reimbursement
for expenses that are either inappropriate or not allowed by this policy. Exceptions to this policy
can only be granted in special situations with the approval of the appropriate Group VP of Finance
or the Corporate CFO.

Employees should neither gain nor lose financially due to business-related travel.
Employees are not permitted to use the company's credit card accounts to make personal travel
reservations or to charge personal expenses.
Employees are required to submit their own expenses related to business travel. In the event of a
group bill the senior employee present must pay and expense the bill. The names of all attendees

RESPONSIBILITES
All employees are responsible for complying with the Company travel and entertainment policies.
Further, employees are responsible for the authenticity and a ccuracy of the contents of their own
expense reports. Incomplete, unsupported or unapproved expense reports will not be processed for
payment.

The managers/approvers are responsible to review and verify all necessary expense information
for employees under their direction. All expenses that do not fall within the guidelines of this
policy need to be disapproved and returned with a comment to the employee to correct or provide
additional information . Once the correction has been made the employee must resubmit the
expense report for approval.

The Company assumes no obligation to reimburse employees for expenses that are not in
compliance with this policy.

ENFORCEMENT
Employees who do not comply with this policy may be subject to delay or withholding of
reimbursement and/or disciplinary action up to and including termination.

All expense reports are subject to audit by Corporate Audit and Assurances Services (CAAS) for
compliance with company policy and verification of proper documentation.

o Fare savings is $200 USD (or equivalent amount in local currency) or more

o One-way elapsed trip time is not increased by more than 2 hours; or
o Departure/arrival time is within 2 hours (before/after) of the requested time.

Employees involved in an accident in a rental car must do the following,
Before leaving the accident scene (to the extent possible) obtain the following
information:
• How, when & where the accident occurred;
• Personal Contact information (telephone number(s) and home street address - if driving on
business obtain the name, telephone number and street address of their employer) from
other people involved in the accident (drivers of other vehicles involved, injured persons,
witnesses and Government Law Enforcement Agency Officers;
• Driver's License information from other driver's involved in the accident;
• Vehicle(s) information for all vehicles involved in the accident from vehicle(s)'
government registration document (year, make, model, vehicle identification number,
license plate number & name contact information of auto insurance company);
• Injured person(s)' injuries (by person's name);
• Property damage to your rental car, other vehicles involved in the accident and other
property damage (not to an auto such as a fence, pole, guardrail, etc.);
• If your rental car is not drivable, the contact information of the tow company and the name
and address where the rental car was taken;
• Government Law Enforcement Agency issued citations / traffic violation tickets;
• Call 911 or another general emergency contact number if there are any injuries involved;
• Take pictures of all documents and property damage; and
• Do not accept any liability or obligation to make a payment to another.

Cell Phone Use and Texting While Driving
Many studies have shown the correlation between driving while distracted (due to talking or
texting on cell phones) and auto accidents. Further, a number of states prohibit or restrict the use
of cell phones and texting while driving. Accordingly, the Company prohibits any use of a cell
phone and texting while driving, unless allowed by law, and even then, discourages such use.

other employee (s), on an out -of-town overnight business trip. The company will reimburse
employees for reasonable and customary meal expenses during a business outing. Meals en route
and at the assignment locations are reimbur sable if considered reasonable based on location and
circumstances.

Business meals are taken with clients and prospects, during which a specific business discussion
takes place. The Company will reimbu rse employees for the reasonable cost of business meals

Receipt Guidelines
Receipt guidelines vary by country. While it is preferable that all receipts be submitted, receipts
are required for all expenses over $25. Receipts must be original and must include sufficient
information to establish the amount, date, place and the essential character of the expenditure.
Receipts must be scanned into the expense reporting

Acceptable receipts include:

• An airline itinerary / invoice with the ticket number or airline passenger coupon.
• A hotel folio (which itemizes all room charges, applicable taxes, phone charges, dining
room charges, etc.).
• An auto rental agreement or the transaction record received upon vehicle return.
• A corporate card charge receipt or a restaurant cash register receipt. Restaurant receipt "tear
tabs" may not be used as receipt.

If a receipt is lost, travelers should provide a written explanation of the expense and the reason for
the missing receipt. This explanation should be included with the receipts required at the time of
expense report submission.

GENERAL TRAVEL SAFETY TIPS

Air Travel
• Plan to arrive at the airport at least one (1) hour before scheduled flight for domestic travel
and two (2) hours for international flights.
• Do not leave luggage unattended or with someone you do not know.
• Never agree to carry anything on board or in your luggage for someone else.
• Put your name and address on your luggage. For added security, use tags that conceal your
name and address.
• Remove all destination tags to avoid misdirection by baggage handlers.
• Carry all medication, important business papers, some toiletries, and a change of clothing
in carry-on luggage in case the checked luggage is misplaced.
• Enroll in your country of citizenship government travel program if you travel
internationally so you can receive benefits such as:
o Important information from the Embassy about safety conditions in your destination
country(s) helping you make informed decisions about your travel plans.
o Help the Embassy contact you in an emergency, whether natural disaster, civil unre st,
or family emergency.
o Help family and friends get in touch with you in an emergency.

Note: Employees who are U.S. citizens and travel abroad on Company business should
create an account in the U.S. Department of State , Bureau of Consular Affairs' "Smart
Traveler Enrollment Program" (STEP). To learn more about t he STEP and to create an
account on line use the following link: https://step.state.gov/step/

Upon Arrival at Your Destination
• Beware of people claiming to be cab drivers in unmarked cars. Use licensed taxi or
limousine services only.
• If renting a car, get maps in advance or from the rental car counter and clearly write
out the directions from the airport to your destination.
• Stop to ask for directions only in well-lit public areas.
• Keep the telephone number of your destination with you.
